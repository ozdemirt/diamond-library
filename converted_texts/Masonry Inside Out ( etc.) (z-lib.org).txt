Forward
Before you read this book, we would like to give some insight into
the proceeding pages. At this point, you may or may not know that
Masonry is a progressive science and often very cryptic. Moreover,
the preceding will show that Masonry is a spiritual journey and a
profound story told backward. If you are reading this and are a new
Master Mason, we know you have so many questions. This book
aims to provide both clarity and understanding regarding the
Fraternity, which you are now a proud member of.
To sum up the aforementioned, we would like to state how you
should be reading this book. Since there are three authors, each
chapter will give you the individual author’s interpretations of each
degree and their personal experience in this great Fraternity.
Further, we wanted to provide some spiritual nuggets you can glean
insight on, especially concerning the first three degrees in Masonry,
e.g., Entered Apprentice, Fellow Craft, and Master Mason. In
closing, you will find at the end three quizzes you can take to see
what you may have learned from your current masonic journey or
your general knowledge of ancient craft masonry.
The Entered Apprentice Degree
From Whence You Came?
As an Entered Apprentice Mason, are a newcomer in a strange land.
By the time an Entered Apprentice is Passed to the Fellow Craft
Degree, or Raised to the Sublime Degree of Master Mason,  you will
already have gained some experience of Masonry, however slight;
you will have come to feel at ease in your new home. As you stand

--- Bölüm/Sayfa 7 ---
ready to pass through the inner door into the Entered Apprentice
Degree, however, you are a man with no memories to draw from,
no precedents to guide yourself,  nothing to go on. Still, all is new
and strange, and it may even be somewhat disconcerting or even
alarming. Nor are you in a much better state after the Degree has
ended. You doubtless, will feel bewildered and wonder what all this
was about and what will come next.
Moreover, you are contemplating the thought of “Where did you
first decide to become a mason?" The standard answer is, in your
heart. This way, I would offer this caveat, which you first decided
in your mind, and that thought manifested itself to your heart and
build sincerity. Hence, this chapter's intended purpose is to discuss
the first step of Masonry so that you will become quickly
acquainted with the First Degree and all that it signifies.
The First Step
What are the three qualities that every Entered Apprentice should
possess to attain Freemasonry’s full benefit and enjoyment? After
receiving the Entered Apprentice Degree, you should have
impressed upon the mind the three qualities that every Entered
Apprentice should possess if you are to attain full benefit and
enjoyment of Freemasonry. These are obedience, humility, and
industriousness. You should comply with those appointed to teach
you to learn what is required of you before progressing further. You
must have a sense of humility, so you will develop respect for and
appreciation of the knowledge of those who are assigned to teach
you. You must also develop a spirit of industry; Freemasonry
requires that those who want to know more about Masonry spend
time learning. To be humble is to be kind and approachable,
coachable and teachable; Mathew 5:5 says that the meek shall
inherit the earth.
Consequently, this is the primary first and most crucial step in
Masonry. Therefore, we take that clue from Boaz. From that source,
we discover that Boaz well to do Bethlehemite in the days of the
judges who became an ancestor of our lord by marrying Ruth, the

Moabites' widow of one of the sons of Elimelech, his kinsman. Boaz
was well off and had every reason to proceed through life with a
pompous attitude, yet he was extremely humble. Therefore, the
previous lesson of humility will then shape the type of mason you
will ultimately become.
Duly and Truly Prepared
What is meant by "duly and truly prepared"? Being duly and truly
prepared refers to wearing special garments furnished by the Lodge
to emphasize our concern with man's internal qualities, rather than
his worldly wealth and honors. By wearing the garments of
humility, the candidate signifies the sincerity of his intentions.
Symbols of Masonry
Most of Freemasonry's great lessons are imparted by symbols that
were carefully selected by our Masonic ancestors. The use of
symbols to teach moral lessons is traced to the earliest known
Masonic lodges. To corollary, this question is posed "What are
symbols" the response; sensible usages of images used to express
hidden meaning. Moreover, Masonry is ever-present in our lives,
and the symbols surround us; we must first "Ask, Seek and then
knock," and the door will be opened to the recesses of our minds to
comprehend the beauty the Most High God has created, Genesis 1:
1-31. To sum up the preceding, the Entered Apprentice Mason must
then understand that "If you want something you never had, you
must do something you have never done" (Robinson, 2020).
What Do You Most Desire?
As a newly made Entered Apprentice, you’ll find similarities
between the teachings of the fraternity and the teachings of your
faith. As you wait with patience, you have ample time to reflect on
why you have chosen to embark on this journey. Whether you were
influenced by mercenary motives and remained quiet during this
time, you were already prepared in your heart and offered yourself
as a candidate for Masonry. Think back on your wait, and

remember how you nervously anticipated the next instruction from
your conductor, as if you firmly gripped that cable-tow.
Your wait with patience has now become synonymous as to the
urgency of a woman anticipating labor. As a mother carries,
protects, and nurtures her unborn, your new family of brothers are
anticipating your arrival as well. Prior to receiving light, you
symbolically remained in the womb, waiting to be birthed into
light, soon to have the umbilical cord, or rather your cable-tow
severed by your conductor. This type of rebirth is required for the
new Apprentice, and he can no longer survive being fed by the
indulgence of his past time in darkness. He must now depend on his
mother to nourish him with milk, with the expectation he will
desire meat, by maturing in the teachings, tenets, wisdom, and rich
history of freemasonry.
As you continue your travels in freemasonry, you must understand
this to be your own experience, and though it may lead you to work
in foreign lands, never forget your reasoning for pursuing this
journey. From knocking to being raised to a living perpendicular,
you made numerous steps with others by your side, but as King
David spoke unto his son, he instructed Solomon to let the word be
a lamp unto his feet and a light unto his path, Psalms 119:105.
Symbolically, this is a charge to all Master Masons. Whether you
adhere to the Christian faith or not, the representative of King
Solomon in your lodge has imputed you with the same words of
wisdom in his various charges to you and the other brothers of your
lodge. Allow the wisdom, tenets, teachings, and brotherly love to be
a lamp unto your feet and a light unto your path.  As you travel this
world with the lamp and light of your VSL that rests on the altar,
utilize the fraternity’s teachings to continue receiving more and
further light to illuminate your path.
This light represents information, wisdom, preparation, direction,
and answers. Again, we are told to seek wisdom in contemplation.
As a fraternity of men, we should seek wisdom, as a young man
seeking the beauty of his eyes, to learn, court, marry, and
symbolically mate with her, producing unbiased judgment,
compassion, self-knowledge, self-awareness, and virtues such as
ethics and benevolence. As a student of freemasonry, you’ll find the

What is True Darkness?
Our motto is probably something that people hear most often about
masons, “Make Good Men Better.”  What does that mean, and why
do we as men want to be better?  This is a question that I had
before becoming a mason and even to this day.  Manly P. Hall states
that all living things at one point in their life realize that they are a
prisoner (1976).  What does that mean, and how are we prisoners
when we are free to make decisions and choices in our lives.  I
believe some of us felt this way during the Covid pandemic, but I
would venture that most men who became Masons and I’ve met had
had this battle long before Covid was around.  We live through it
and are drawn to free ourselves of this barrier and the chains that
hold us back from becoming better versions of ourselves.  This is
what enticed me into becoming a mason.  One of my lifelong
friends told me that he was a mason.  I was pleased and also
confused, as I did not know what a true mason was.  I noticed that
he made a massive shift in his life and was conducting himself
differently, for good.  I’m not saying that he was terrible or even
someone that was on the wrong path, just that small things shifted
in him that shined through.  I wanted to join to be a part of
something more prominent in my life and provide more of a
purpose.
I was a prisoner in my own body and mind.  I was successful and, at
the same time, trapped in who I could potentially be. I wanted more
but didn’t know how to get there or had the confidence to go it
alone.  This is one of the first things that I learned when being
prepared for the first time.  You can’t go it alone; you must follow
your conductor.  I needed to be placed in the darkness to heighten
the other senses that I have grown to ignore.  I needed to hear, be
in a place of safe fear and trust.  Trust is something that we, as men,

--- Bölüm/Sayfa 12 ---
tend to hold near and dear to our hearts.  We only let some into this
circle of trust and forcibly remove or restrict others from gaining
entrance.
A significant question comes from this; how do we openly and
sincerely trust others while in darkness?  In my career as an
educator, facilitating transformational life and academic programs
internationally, there was one activity that made a significant
impact on everyone. It was called a blind trust walk.  In this
activity, we learn to place your trust in someone else to guide you
safely around, over, and under obstacles.  There are moments of
fear, nervous laughter, and a feeling of calm.  The more you
participate in this activity with success, the more comfortable it
becomes.  The more comfortable it becomes, the more risks the
facilitator has the pairs take. The greater the risks, the greater the
learning and growth potential; however, this cannot happen
immediately.  It takes time and lessons learned in darkness as well
as in the light.
As an entered apprentice, you will go through times of darkness,
and in the beginning, it will feel weird and different.  The more you
travel, the easier it becomes to walk in darkness.  The more
comfortable we are in darkness, the brighter we can shine in the
light.  We are the only ones that know what we are battling in the
darkness and if you choose to share this with a Brother, know that
they will be there to help guide you through it.  When we were first
prepared in the anteroom, we learned a valuable lesson.  Wait.
Wait in darkness; learn to be peaceful in that darkness.  Learn to
lean on others in that darkness.  It is a beautiful experience that if I
could go through it all over with a different mindset, I believe I
would have had a profound understanding of what it all meant for
me as a man.
Back to our original question, what is true darkness?  True darkness
is what we are going through in our lives at the various stages and
places when we enter it.  Our ability to learn from this darkness is
what separates us from the masses.  We have traveled this path
before with different starting and endpoints.  We have learned to
put our trust in others and the knowledge that we cannot travel this
path alone.  Once we master our pursuit of light, there can no

--- Bölüm/Sayfa 13 ---
longer be darkness.  For wherever there is light, darkness cannot
exist.  Only you will know, and only you will be able to understand
when your light is bright enough to combat darkness.  Myself, I am
still learning and growing.  I am building my source so that I can be
resourceful in the future.  Travel light, my Brothers, travel Light.

The Fellow Craft Degree
Are you a Fellow Craft?
The word fellow derives from the Anglo-Saxon phrase felaw .
Further, the fore-mentioned derives from two words, fe and loy ,
which signifies bound in mutual trust, a plausible derivative, not
necessarily unsuited to the word's meaning. Moreover, the better
etymology derives from its Anglo-Saxon folgian , or “ to follow,” and
thus fellow would hence be considered a follower , a companion , an
associate . As we continue, the fellowcraft degree is a symbol for the
stage of both adulthood and responsibility as it pertains to man's
life while here on earth. Freemasonry deals in terms of education
lent from the liberal arts and sciences. For one to be a fellow craft,
mason must be urged to advance his knowledge and seek to be paid
his wages. Consequently, as one progresses with being a fellow
craft, they seek to earn their wages.
What do we mean; on the six-hour of the six-day of the week,
Mathew 20: 5 describes those same fellow crafts, in just of the text
labors being paid their wages for a day's labor. Hence, we can
concede that there were two levels of fellow craft at the building
and sustainment of King Solomon's Temple, contrary to popular
opinion and belief. The fellowcraft degree dwells in the recesses of
the mind and its faculties. The fellowcraft is instructed in the seven
liberal arts and sciences. As mentioned above, those are formulated
hundreds of years ago, merely to order and to cultivate the
development of one's perfect mental nature. To be a fellow craft and
understand the teachings therein contained, prepare the mind for
spiritual truths.
From 1725 to 1738, there were only two degrees of Masonry:
Entered Apprentice and Fellow Craft. The majority of all business
was conducted primarily in the Entered Apprentice Degree. As it is

the radical meaning of the word is a fellow workman . This way, the
showing of the origin of the title originates for an operative
institution. The working tools of a fellow craft are the square, level,

The Ascent
Perfection on earth has never yet been obtained. Even the wisest
and best of men have all gone astray. The notion that we are
Masters of our profession is somewhat naive, and during this
degree, we realize that, as fellow crafts, we must apply this new

--- Bölüm/Sayfa 17 ---
knowledge in the discharge of the duties that we owe to God. We
could also state that we have mastered our tools’ understanding and
employment, such as circumscribing our actions via the compass,
subduing our passions, and breaking off the superfluous corners
with the common gavel, attempting to transform ourselves from the
rough ashlar taken from the quarry of the world in our natural
state, to being reshaped into a smooth ashlar, fitting us as living
stones for the Master use. Arriving at this degree, we begin to
realize that we are to remain fellow crafts in this existence, working
to perfect ourselves and our points of entry, which are the spiritual
signs, tokens, and passwords for access to the celestial lodge above.
As we transverse this existence, we understand that we are
continually striving for perfection. Although we received the
working tools to break off superfluous corners of rough stone, the
trial we face, and the obstacles we overcome, we are being
perfected by learning to subdue our passions, seeking wisdom.
While she may be found, and equally dividing our time through
discipline, we are discharging the duties we owe to God. The charge
in the Fellow Craft degree reminds us that it’s the internal and not
the external qualifications of a man is what masonry regards.
 As we pursued our journey from the pillars to the first three steps,
we learned that as entered apprentices, we ought industriously to
occupy our minds in the attainment of useful knowledge. As Fellow
Crafts, we should apply our knowledge to the discharge of these
duties. Therefore, we are not taught to study for knowledge alone,
but for the purpose of applying this knowledge in assisting our
neighbors, aiding and relieving every brother that needs it, which
means you are an ambassador of the Creator.  As we are given the
letter G lecture, we begin to understand how Freemasonry’s
symbolism impresses on the neophyte’s mind, the mysteries that
were revealed to him, the various charges, and the various truths of
the fraternity.

and what was explained to you. You learned that one explanation of

You were taught that by geometry, we might curiously trace nature
through her various windings to her most concealed recesses

Why Winding Stairs?
“When I was a child, I spake as a child, I understood as a child, I
thought as a child: but when I became a man, I put away childish
things.” - 1st Corinthians 13:11
Why the winding stairs, and what do they represent in our lives?
To understand this fully, we need to break down what each flight
means and how that applies to our lives.  First and foremost, the
winding flight of stairs consisting of 3,5 and 7 steps mean more in
their construction than what is taught in the lodge.  Think about it
this way: why isn’t the flight of 3, 5, and 7 steps straight instead of
winding?  That is what we will endeavor to learn in this lecture.
The three steps allude to the principal stages of human life,
principal supports, and principal officers.  In the last chapter, we
spoke about the stage of youth. In this chapter, we talk about the
stage of manhood, and in the next chapter, we will talk about the
stage of old age.  But why was masonry broken down into these
stages?  The easiest way to describe this is that it held relevance to
all and would hold the same relevance for the future.  Remember
that our ancient brethren were not near sited. They planned for the
big picture and looked into developing and building structures that
would stand the test of time.  They had the wisdom to contrive
what was laid down on their trestle board.  They built structures of
strength that has been adorn by all that followed them.  Imagine
what your life would be like if you spent the time to plan out what
your temporal structure would look like.  If we applied this
knowledge to our lives, how much better would we live and thrive
in our daily intercourse with others?
The five steps provide an outline of the choices that we have in
building our temporal building.  There are five orders of
architecture. You get to choose which one is your style and go with
it.  Be ready to understand who is there to help you and who isn’t
there to help you.  Use your senses and be aware of your
surroundings.  I know that I can trust my brother and identify him
with signs, words, and grips at night or in the day.  These are

continue to build my temple.  They will remind me if I am messing
up and help me rebuild my temple if possible.  Put your trust in
them because they have earned it by going through what we all
have gone through.
The seven steps describe how to use the liberal arts and sciences to
build our temporal building and occupy our minds with knowledge
and understanding.  We know that from the seven steps that we will
be without at times in our lives, we can push through this and still
build our temple to be a wonder of this world.  The more we learn
about Geometry, synonymous with Masonry, the more we
understand the world’s happenings and how we can use these forces
to our benefit.  I encourage you to dive into your ritual and read
through the middle chamber.  Then apply those lessons to your life
and see how it begins to shift.
All of this is impossible without this one thing, trust.  We must trust
that the lessons being taught are meant for us and those like us.  We
must trust that those that have come before us laid down plans for
us to prosper and not dwindle.  We must trust that God has us and
is with us.  This is why we have a winding staircase instead of a
straight staircase.  When you have a winding staircase, you can’t see
what is coming and must trust that you will be safe, continue to
grow, and make your ascent from youth to manhood to old age.
Trust your guide and fear no danger.
