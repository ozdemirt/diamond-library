BÜYÜK ÜSTAT MESAJI
Değerli Kardeşlerim,
Tekrisimizde bize en başta sorulan " ... siz, Tanrıya
inanıyor musunuz?" sorusu; masonik yaşamı­
mızın ve dolayısıyla eğitim ve öğretimizin te­
melini teşkil eder. Bu soruya verdiğimiz cevap­
la, "Evrenin Ulu Mimarı", "Yaradan" ve 'Tüce
Varlık" olarak kabul ettiğimiz Tanrıya ve var­
lığına inancımızı göstermiş oluruz.
Tekrisimizde Nur"a kavuşmamızın akabinde, Loca­
larımızın "evreni simgelediği" bize verilen ilk
derslerdendir. Şu halde biz eğitimimizi evreni
simgeleyen bir boyutta yapmaktayız.
Eğitim ve öğretimimizi gördüğümüz Localarımızın
evreni simgelediğini ve evreni yaratanın da
Tanrı olduğunu kabul ettiğimize göre; Locaları-

mız sembolik yönden Tanrının yarattığı bir
mekândır. Bunun için, bu mekânın, diğer de­
yimle Localarımızın aynı zamanda bir "Mabet"
olduğunu kabul ederiz.
Evrenin Ulu Mimarı olan Yüce Varlığın
yarattığı
varlıklardan birisi de bizleriz. Ancak Tanrı bizi
yarattığı evrenin kuvvet ve kudretini, maddi
manevi değerlerini, güzelliklerini görüp değer­
lendirip benimseyelim ve paylaşalım diye ya­
ratmıştır. Bir Mason olarak bunun aksini
düşünmek mümkün değildir. Bunun için de
Akıl ve Hikmet bize yol gösterecektir. Bu ne­
denle, evreni simgeleyen Localarımızda Kutsal
Kitaplar üzerinde Gönye ve Pergel bulun­
maktadır.
Masonluk bir yaşam biçimi olduğu ve bu Masonik
yaşam ile ilgili eğitimin Localarda yapılabilece­
ği kabul edildiğine göre; masonik yaşantımıza
ilişkin eğitim ve öğretimimizdeki düşünceleri­
mizin, diğer düşüncelerimizden çok daha fark­
lı olması gerekir.
Kısaca açıkladığım bu tespitlerim ışığında düşünce­
lerimizin, evrensel boyutlarda olması halinde,
bir masonik düşünce olarak kabulü mümkün
olabilecektir. Şu halde, Hakikati arayışımızda
düşüncelerimizin evrensel olması gereklidir.
Bu nedenle de, düşüncelerimiz ile evrensel bo­
yutları benimseyip yaklaşabildiğimiz oranda
da tekâmül yolunda aşama kaydedebilir ve
nihayet bir Mason olabiliriz.
Bir Mason olarak bizlerin de; Evrenin Ulu Mima­
rı'mn evren içinde yarattığı birer varlık ve
evrenin bir parçası olduğumuza göre; birbiri­
mizle olan ilişkilerde de düşünce ve davranış­
larımızın, evrensel boyutlarda olması değer­
lendirilmesi lazımdır. Aksi takdirde, Hakikati
yakalama ve görebilme olanağımız olamaya­
caktır.

MESSAGE FROM THE GRAND MASTER
Dear Brethren,
During our Initiation Ceremony, the first question
we were asked "do you believe in God?"
forms the basement of our Masonic life and
education. With our favourable reply to this
question we point forward our belief and faith
to the presence of God whom we call "The
Great Architect of the Universe" , 'The Creator"
or the "Supreme Being".
Upon receipt of "the Light" during this ceremony,
the first lesson we are fought is that our Temple
represents "the Universe". Thus, we
are
continuing our education in a dimension which
symbolises the universe.
We consent that our Temples, where we carry on
our
masonic
education,
symbolises
the
universe created by God ; symbolicaly our
Lodges are also premises created by God.
Therefore we too accept that these premises, in
other words, our Lodges are "Temples" as
well.
The Supreme Being who is The Great Architect of
the Universe, has created us, the human be­
ings. However, God has created us to realise

the strength and power of the universe, He has
created, with all its moral aspects and beauty.
It is us who have to appreciate and share these
values. As a Mason, it is not possible to think of
the opposite. The Intelligence and Wisdom will
lead us in this path. For this reason, the Square
and the Compasses are placed on the open
Volume's of the Sacred Law in our Temples,
symbolising the universe.
In our general opinion Masonry is a way of life and
the masonic education can only be received in
the Temples, the thoughts and ideas related to
our
Masonic
life
and
education
must
completely
be different
than
our
other
thoughts and ideas.
In brief, under the light of my above
deter­
minations, our thoughts and ideas can only be
deemed as Masonic on condition that they are
in universal dimensions. Thus, in our quest for
the Truth, our ideas have to be
universal.
Therefore, we shall only be able to improve
ourselves if we can catch the universal di­
mensions by our ideas and adopt them as our
way of life and finally become a Freemason.
We Freemasons are a part of this universe and its
Creator, the Great Architect. From this stand
point, our mutual thoughts and ideas, our
relations and our behaiours amongst us must
also be evaluated
in universal norms and
dimensions. Otherwise,
our efforts in our
quest for finding the Truth will be useless.
I wish that the Great Architect will give my Brethren
the strength and power during our quest to
reach our target by searching the good, the
correct and the beautiful
and be able to
upgrade
our thoughts
coordinating
our
behaviours
and ideas, towards
universal
levels and values.
Demir
SAVAŞÇIN
Grand Master

UNKNOWN FOUNDING LODGE OF THE
GRAND LODGE OF TURKEY : BARK AT (JAFFA)
The Grand Lodge of Turkey was found in 1909 by 12 lodges existing then in the
Ottoman Empire, attached to Italian, French (Grand Orient), Spanish and Egyptian
Grand Lodges plus lodges attached to the Ottoman Supreme Council (founded in
1861). German, English, Scottish, Irish, Hungarian, Greek and Polish lodges did not
participate.
In our archives, by chance, a correspondence, dated from November 1909 to
February 1910, between Lodge BarkaV and the Grand Orient of France was found.
From this correspondence the following emerged:

a. Barkai Lodge (Jaffa) was one of the founding Lodges of the Grand Lodge of Turkey.
The delegate of the lodge to the founding council in Istanbul was Bro. Shukru
Abdelnur.
b. Barkai' Lodge was using a Christian motto (In Hoc Signo Vinces<
2))accompanied
by a cross on the heading of its letter paper. The new Grand Lodge of Turkey asked
Lodge Barkai to replace these symbols "of the enemy of humanity" with masonic
symbols.
c. The Grand Orient of France, which did not yet recognize the Grand Lodge of
Turkey, approved the resolution of the Grand Lodge of Turkey and asked also the
lodge to change the heading of its letter paper.
d. The Grand Orient pointed that a Lodge could belong to only one obedience and
asked Barkai to chose between France and Turkey. Barkai' chose to stay with France
and severed its ties to the Grand Lodge of Turkey.
e. Lodge Barkai asked the Grand Orient to recognize the young Ottoman Grand
Orient at their earliest convenience, as the establishment of regular Masonry could
only help the development of all the social classes in the Ottoman Empire.
f. Lodge Barkai asked the Grand Orient a permission for the Brothers to participate
in the labor of the Turkish Lodges, which were not yet recognized.
g. Lodge Barkai asked the Grand Orient to intervene in Istanbul near the Minister of
the Interior Talat Pasha<
3', Grand Master of the Grand Lodge of Turkey, against the
deputy of Jaffa who was intriguing against Freemasonry. [Most of the members of
the Party "Union and Progress" (Ittihad ve Terakki), which was in power, were
Freemasons. The constitutional revolution had been prepared in the secrecy of
Masonic Lodges. The period from 1909 to 1918 had been named by the French
historian Thierry Zarcone "Masonic Government".] The reactionary deputy of Jaffa
wanted to promote an antimasonic propaganda to decrease support to the new
democratic government.

HELLO PHILOSOPHY-III: WHAT IS AESTHETICS?:
Beauty is a concept Freemasons come across at least twice during each Lodge
meeting. It is a concept that gives meaning to the Junior Warden's column. The
theme is beauty and/or the aesthetics, a concept which can be evaluated in terms of
philosophy. What do we understand from the word "beauty"? "The best",
"Competent" , "High morals" or "Very pleasant"? Although these qualities are in
harmony with each other, they contradict one another at the same time. We usually
have rational and logical relations with the objects around us, but at the same time
approach them from an aesthetic point of view. We, human beings are not satisfied
with the environment that we are bom into, but we also live in a world that we create
ourselves. We have a cultural world apart from the natural one in which arts play an
important role. Just as we look at our natural surroundings, we look at arts from an
aesthetic point of view and evaluate each object as beautiful or ugly. Is there a
universal concept for "beauty" in nature or in a work of art? This is an ongoing
argument; however, we know that there has never been an absolute beauty in any
period of time...
