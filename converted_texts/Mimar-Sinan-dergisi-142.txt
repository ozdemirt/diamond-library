MESSAGE FROM THE GRAND MASTER
Dear
Brethren,
T o l e r a n c e is a featuring
virtue
of our masonic
craft.
It can
be
expressed
as "meeting
an idea
or a view
in
tranquility
with
respect
and
without
showing
any
reaction"
which
consists
of two
main
senses
:
- To show
respect
to one's
view
although
it may
be
totally
contrary
to his
own,
- To show
respect
to all views
and
ideas
accepted
by
the
whole
society
such as the rules and laws
of the
country
or to all
religions.
The Tolerance
virtue
first started
and became
prevalent
with
the
humanism
activities
in the
Western
Europe
and
added
a lot
to the
formation
of
dialectic.
However,
Tolerance
shows
no respect
to views
and
ideas
which
tend
to limit
or constrain
free
thinking.
In the
core
of
Tolerance
exists
the principal
of " all views
and ideas
can
be explained,
a hierarchy
of values
between
these
ideas
can not be installed
and no view
or idea can be
judged".
The word
T o l e r a n c e must
not be confounded
with
the
word
I n d u l g e n c e . As a meaning
indulgence
has a tendency
to
forgive
deeds
or behaviours
or even guilts
which
can
not
be
accepted
by
the
society.
A
lawyer
expects
the
indulgence
of the Judge
for his guilty
client.
The
wrong
deed
of a child
can be indulged
if it will
not lead
to a
weakness
or a crime
in his future.
There is a wrong
deed
or a behaviour
in indulgence.
However,
tolerance
has
nothing
to do with
any
mistake.
Indulgence
is a long
debated
issue
in Freemasonry.
For
example,
can we indulge
or forgive
behaviours
against
our masonic
rules,
principals
or landmarks
?
According
to my own
personal
view;
No! Since we are not a
group
of children,
any
kind
of action
or behaviour
which
will
become
an
example
after
some
time
will
lead
Freemasonry
to weakness
and
therefore
can
not
be
indulged
or forgiven.
However,
I do not
want
to
go
further
into
indulgence,
but
would
like
to
emphasize
more
on
"tolerance".

MASONLUK NEDİR?
Tanju KORAY
WHAT IS FREEMASONRY?
When we look at the Masonic World today, we see that there are around 150
Grand Lodges and about 4,5 million Freemasons. According to a brochure
published by the United Grand Lodge of England in 1984, it is expressed that:
Freemasonry
is the most ancient
secular brotherhood
association
of the World.
It is
an association
of gentlemen
who respect moral and ethical
virtues. The three main
tenets of Freemasonry can be stated as Brotherly Love, Charity and the Search for
the Divine Truth. Furthermore, Freemasonry asks all its members to respect and
obey the laws and rules of the country they live in. Like all esoteric organisations,
Freemasonry has its own secrets but is not a secret society. All the rules and
regulations of the body is open to public and any member is free to announce that
he is a Freemason. Freemasons have promised to work for the benevolence of
mankind. They gather and work in autonomous groups called Lodges. They show
special care and respect to their traditions, regulations, landmarks as well as their
written and unwritten rules while working for their craft. A Freemason gives no
importance to the race, nationality, religion or the social or political status of any
member. All brethren are equal with a unique condition that every member must
express a belief in a Supreme Being. His religion has no importance but all religions
gain great respect by all the members. In short, Freemasonry is a brotherhood of
men who strive with all their might to make things better in the world.

FREEMASONRY OF THE MOORS
The origins of Freemasonry - as would be expected of such a "venerable secret
society" - are shrouded in myth, legend and almost impenetrable obscurity. Since at
least the late 18th century Masonic writers have sought to establish a link between
the Knights Templar and the Freemasons. Freemasonic lore and symbols have been
traced to ancient Egypt and Phoenicia. Yet, despite all the books and articles
exploring Freemasonry published over the last hundred years, there is one area that
has not received attention. It concerns Freemasonry's debt to Islamic mysticism and
a shadowy tradition connecting the Masons with the Moors of North Africa. The
Moors provided the vital link between ancient and modern civilization. The light of
knowledge which illuminated the Moorish lands of Spain and Sicily was
instrumental in dispelling the gloom of ignorance that enveloped mediaeval
Europe.

"THE INITIATION CEREMONY OF VOLTAIRE"
With this article, we carry on publishing the old documents in the Grand Lodge
Library.
The subject of our current article is a pamphlet published in 1925 in Istanbul with a
title of "The Initiation Ceremony of Voltaire". The text is actually a conference by a
member of WL Vefa. The author, Bro. Hikmet, explains in the foreword that he has
found a biography of the French philosopher Voltaire in a friend's library. While
studying the book, he found the minutes of the initiation ceremony of Voltaire. He
translated this chapter and gave a conference on this subject. The pamphlet
contains this conference.
In this article we first give the transliteration of the pamphlet which is in Arabic
script. Then because of the dramatic changes in the Turkish language in the last
century we also give the text, which contains a lot of Arabic and Persian words and
idioms in today's Turkish.
The author, Bro. Hikmet has autographed this issue to a new EA Bro. Muhittin Celal.
At the back cover of the pamphlet, new EA Bro. Muhittin Celal wrote down his
impressions on his own initiation. Bro. Muhittin Celal will later become the Grand
Secretary of the Grand Lodge in mid 30's. So this issue has another unique
