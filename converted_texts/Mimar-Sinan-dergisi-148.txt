Researchers into the history of Freemasonry in the Middle East usually skip a beat
when they find a book about what had appeared in the past. Books written in the
past about Freemasonry have all disappeared. The history, which should have been
written and documented, is now forgotten and lost, if not purposefully obliterated.
The surprise is manifold as the book in question is one of the oldest written, and is
not one vilifying Freemasonry, old and modern, which one can still find on the
shelves of many bookshops. Such a surprise was in store for me, when I first set eyes
on what is certainly one of tile oldest, if not the oldest, published in the Arabic
language about Freemasonry. In the early eighties, I was serving in Egypt in the
diplomatic service of Israel when in an old collection of books, offered for sale, I
found the book. It was a wonderful surprise. The title of the hard-cover book is
moderate: "General History of Masonry" and the sub-title "From its early days till
today." The author is Georgy Zeidan, a very famous historian-novelist, whose

TALAT PASHA
From the Constitutional Monarchy to the Foundation of the Grand Lodge
Talat Pasha was born in 1874 in Edirne. After graduating from the Military School he
had to start work upon the loss of his father. After working for five years as a postman
he was appointed as the head clerk of the Post Office in 1903. He spent most of his
time for the improvement and prevalence of the Union and Progress Committee.
History records that most of the members of the this Committee were freemasons
and played an important role in the declaration of the Second Constitutional
Monarchy on 23rd July 1908. The famous watchword "Freedom, Equality and
Fraternity" was the motto of all Union and Progress Committee members. Talat
Pasha was attending the regular lodge meetings and was trying to spread the idea of
"Freedom" especially among the young population of his country. Being an
effective member of the Union and Progress Committee and due to his efforts in the
declaration of freedom, he became a very well known person and was elected from
Edirne to enter the Parliament on July 30, 1908. When the First World War ended in
1918 he supported Mustafa Kemal Pasha for his achievement of the Independence
War. He is known as a decent, modest, rational and determined person. He has
achieved an important contribution in the organizational structure of the Union
and Progress Committee and made a lot studies in the modernisation of his country.
He played an important role in the Parliament for the application of laws and
regulations in the fields of art, women rights, secularism and education.
Unfortunately, this man of enlightment was assasinated on March 15, 1921 in
Berlin.

This article unassumingly tries to suggest that initiation is not an isolated one off
event but, rather a lifelong continuous process that makes use of universal symbols
and allegories and refers to perennial archetypes in order to have the initiated
obtain awareness and conciousness of his own self as well as of his environment
and take the necessary steps on the road towards the quest for truth. The article then
goes on to elaborate in some detail the guidelines of the entered apprentice, the
fellowcraft and the master mason degrees, in the aim of building a Temple of Ideals.

FOUNDATIONS OF MASONIC LAW
The history of law and law making is the history of civilization. In the narro­
wer sense, Masonic law rests upon the Old Constitutions, the Old Charges
and the Landmarks; the superstructure is made up of the Constitutions and
By-Laws of Grand Lodges; the decisions of Grand Lodges on appeals; the
edicts of Grand Masters; the decisions of Grand Masters, sometimes stan­
ding without review, more often reviewed and confirmed by Grand Lodges.
But in the wider sense, Masonic law is based upon English law - which goes
back to Roman law - so that it is within the facts to say that Masonic law is a
development of the ideas of equity, and the administration of justice, of the
days of ancient Rome.
