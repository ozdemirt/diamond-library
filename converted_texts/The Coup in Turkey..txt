Introduction

During the evening hours of Friday, July the 15th, tanks blocked the Bosporus bridges in
Istanbul. Soldiers and armored personnel carriers showed up on the streets of Ankara and
Istanbul while the F-16s roared over. Most people initially thought a significant terror threat
existed and the Military was intervening. By midnight, a speaker read the coup declaration on the
state TV, TRT (Turkish Radio Television).  The announcement explained the aim was to realign
the country with democratic and constitutional norms and to revive Ataturk‟s principles. The
broadcast lasted only for 30 minutes.
The Government looked well-prepared. The police and elements of the Military were quickly
mobilized against the dissenters. Erdogan and Prime Minister showed up on the screens and told
a limited group of Gulenists in the Military were carrying out a coup attempt. They urged millions
to invade the squares and streets to stop the rebellions. Several generals and officers, mostly
suspects of the previous Ergenekon and Balyoz (in English, Sledgehammer) coup plots against
the Government, acted in support of Erdogan. By the dawn of July 16th, all alleged dissenters
decided to surrender. Despite the surrender, pro-Erdogan militias lynched several soldiers. Pro-
Erdogan generals and officers murdered many of them. Bloody confrontations resulted in nearly
200 lost lives. The Government claimed a clear victory against the coupists
Mass arrests throughout the country immediately begun 1 . Thousands of judges and
prosecutors, including two members of the Supreme Court, were the first in line. On 20th of July,
Erdogan declared the State of Emergency and announced a series of Government dictated laws.
The authorities of the police and the judiciary expanded tremendously. Erdogan promised not to
prolong the State of Emergency for more than three months. After almost two years, the State of
Emergency is still in effect because it provides Erdogan unconstrained power. Changing
democracy into tyranny lets him suppress his opponents and extend his rule.
A press release by the Turkish Military2 told that the dissented personnel amounted to 8,651,
mostly mobilized in Istanbul and Ankara. Almost half of them were cadets and conscripts.
However, the crackdown was devastating. Erdogan‟s regime jailed more journalists than all the
other countries of the world. His Government sacked 151,967 government employees, detained

According to an official declaration by the Turkish Military following the events, only 8,651
military personnel involved in the alleged coup attempt (5,761 professional military personnel,

professionals, 270,970 conscripts, and others)150. The announcement also stated only a few types
of equipment from the total inventory were employed. Namely, 35 military aircraft (including
fighter jets and transport aircraft), 37 military helicopters (including eight attack helicopters), 246
armored vehicles (including 74 tanks), three naval ships, and 3992 light weapons were utilized.

helicopters. Turkey has a Police force of 250,000 police officers and a Gendarmerie force of
270,000 gendarmes. It is reasonable to claim the attempt had no chance of success at all
because of the limited number of 8,651 dissenters versus the overwhelming number of the
security forces confronted.

Conclusion

It is not realistic to name the events of the 15th of July as a genuine coup attempt. Erdogan‟s
partners in government departments were well prepared and versed to aggravate the
circumstances and agitate the crowds. Multiple police units acted preemptively before the coup
attempt initiated. Certain Police chiefs provoked the people to break into military headquarters
and lynch the soldiers. Covert operations by SADAT and pro-Erdogan militia cost many lives.
Their goal was to set up bloody confrontations and increase the death toll. As a result, almost
200 people lost their lives.
Akar and Erdogan‟s partners in the Military prioritized individual benefits over the democracy
and the future of the country. Their actions present strong indicators about their prior awareness
and preparation for the day. Without the leadership by Akar and the service chiefs, it is
impossible to carry out a coup in the Turkish Military. As this study shows, Akar and several high
ranking military officials, at least initially, pretended as if they supported the coup attempt. In
fact, the served Erdogan‟s plan of setting up a fake coup. Some generals like Aksakalli and Cetin
even killed their comrades for the benefits they expected from Erdogan. Several generals and
officers, who are the suspects in the Ergenekon, Balyoz and Military Espionage cases in previous
years, also participated in the scheme. They acted with feelings of resentment and revenge and
contributed to the bloodshed.
Despite Akar‟s and Erdogan‟s other collaborators‟ efforts, the number of the military
personnel involved in the events stayed limited and did not conform to a homogeneous or
ideological group. The participation from the Air Force and the Army remained extremely low.
The Navy had no direct involvement in the events. Purposive statements like 'F-16 pilots bombed
their Parliament' do not represent the reality. In fact, the soldiers trapped had to use minimal
force to ensure survival and prevent bloody confrontations. The shootings from F-16s and
helicopters were directly related to the ambushes against the alleged dissenters on the ground.
The events of 15 July 2016 could be best described as a Self-Coup or an Autogolpe. The
definitions and the criteria developed by the experts apply well to the case. Farcau, in his book
„The coup: tactics in the seizure of power‟ describes the Latin American term „Autogolpe‟;
“A coup hunched by the chief executive himself to extend his control over
the political system in some extra-constitutional way (Farcau, 1994: 2)156.”

156 Farcau, B. W. (1994). The coup: tactics in the seizure of power. Westport, Conn: Praeger.

Brooker explains how a military aligns itself with the leader of the country, who plans an
Autogolpe;
“The military‟s support for a civilian President‟s autogolpe -and for his
resulting populist presidential monarchy- could be viewed in similar forensic
fashion to the military‟s seizure of power. But in this case the military is acting as
only an accomplice „before and after the fact‟ in the President‟s misappropriation
of his public office and powers (Brooker, 2014: 102) 157.”

On the 15th of July, Erdogan elaborately designed the scene so that he would have a solid
groundwork for his subsequent political moves. Akar and Erdogan‟s other partners in the Military
provided him with a golden opportunity to consolidate his dictatorial regime and redesign the
country. Erdogan calls this a gift from God. Anyangwe, in his work „Revolutionary Overthrow of
Constitutional Orders in Africa,‟ suggests that Erdogan‟s gift could cost him a gloomy final.  ;
“There have been cases where an incumbent government, tacitly or expressly aided and
abetted by the military, assumes extraconstitutional powers, completely altering the character of
the existing constitutional order and ushering in a totally different one. The incumbent government
in effect overthrows the existing constitutional order and brings in a different one. This situation is
sometimes denoted as a „self-coup.‟ A „self-coup‟ always has the tendency of breeding a rebellion
in the form of a counter-coup or an insurgency, leading to the ouster of the self-coup maker and
the restoration of the status quo ante (Anyangwe, 2012: 48 - 49)158."

The Authors of this study firmly believe that a promising future for Turkey is only possible by
establishing an authentic democracy, sincerely acknowledging the rule of law, and respecting the
dignity and the freedoms of individuals.
